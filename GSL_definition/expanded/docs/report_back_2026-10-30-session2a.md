## 1. Sites recovered/changed by the GPP-aware CUT fallback

a22e893's VUT/CUT fallback was keyed to QC availability alone (`scripts/04_qc.R`'s rule): a site
was "VUT" if it had *any* non-NA `NEE_VUT_REF_QC`, even if `GPP_NT_VUT_REF` was entirely NA for
that site — which then surfaced downstream as a false "no GPP data" drop. The new rule
(`EXP_CFG_CUT_FALLBACK_USE_GPP`, default `TRUE`) requires both the QC column *and* the matching
GPP column to have data before choosing VUT, falling back to CUT only if CUT has both, else
dropping the site outright.

- **153 sites**, up from a22e893's **151** (net +2).
- **AU-Cow and AU-Stp are recovered** — exactly AU-Cow as the prompt expected, plus AU-Stp as a
  bonus (both had VUT QC but all-NA VUT GPP_NT, and both have usable CUT GPP_NT: AU-Cow's 603
  non-NA `GPP_NT_CUT_REF` values were already flagged as unreached by the old rule in the
  original expanded-analysis report-back).
- **5 further sites flip their assigned column** (CL-SDF, CN-You, MX-Tes, RU-SkP, US-xPU — VUT→CUT)
  but still don't make the final 153 (fail rule 4 or rule 5 even under the corrected column).
- **41 population sites are now explicitly dropped** for having no usable VUT *or* CUT column at
  all (neither has both QC and GPP) — a22e893 never flagged these as a distinct category; they
  used to silently resolve to a VUT (or CUT) label with an all-NA GPP column and get caught later
  by the "zero non-NA GPP" drop rule, with no record of *why* no fallback worked. AU-Rob, GF-Guy
  and ID-PaB (named in the original report-back as having neither VUT nor CUT GPP) are among them.
- Full list, per site: `tables/cut_fallback_changes.csv`.

- All 505 GSL-62 site-years remain included (checked directly); US-Ha1 still keeps 17 years.

## 2. Winter partitioning-artifact screen

A day is screened (`GPP_NT_REF` forced to 0, before every derived quantity) if `GPP_NT_REF>0`,
`NEE_REF>=0`, `TA_F<0`°C and `GPP_DT_REF` < 10% of the site's **unscreened** P99 — all four
non-missing; a missing value in any of the four leaves the day unscreened. This is the main
run from here on (`EXP_CFG_WINTER_SCREEN=TRUE`); an unscreened variant (`main_unscreened`) is
kept alongside in every table that has a `variant` column, reproducing a22e893's behaviour.

- **45,236 days screened, across 120 of 153 sites** (not just DBF/MF — any site with cold,
  low-but-positive winter `GPP_NT_REF` that disagrees with `GPP_DT_REF` qualifies; most-affected
  are evergreen/deciduous forest at high latitude: RU-Fyo (1803 d), FI-Hyy (1791 d), US-NR1
  (1549 d), JP-Api (1545 d), US-Ha1 (1457 d)). This is far more than session 1 check 1's 65
  US-Ha1 winter days, because session 1 restricted to Dec–Mar and to days already above a GSL
  threshold; this screen has neither restriction — it applies everywhere `TA_F<0` and the other
  three conditions hold, which reaches shoulder-season days too.
- **Per-site mean Z17 GSL moves by a mean of -1.0 d, median -0.17 d** among the 120 affected
  sites (range -10.6 to 0 d; it can only move GSL down or leave it unchanged, since screening
  only ever removes previously-included days). `delta_P99` is 0 for every site checked by hand
  (RU-Fyo, FI-Hyy, US-NR1, JP-Api, US-Ha1) — screened days are concentrated well below the 99th
  percentile, so P99 itself is essentially unaffected; this is a built-in consequence of the
  screen's own < 10%-of-P99 criterion, not a coincidence.
- **Only 2 sites change skewness third**: RU-Fyo (0.680→0.663, middle stays middle but moves
  toward "low") and JP-Fhk (0.679→0.673) — both already near a tercile boundary; neither actually
  crosses into a different third (`third_changed=FALSE` for both once the screened-run's own
  breaks are used — see item 3).
- Full per-site table: `tables/winter_screen_site_effect.csv`; per-day: `tables/winter_screen_days.csv`;
  per-site-year counts: `tables/winter_screen_site_year.csv`.
- Figure 1a now shows winter-screened days as "neither" (grey) at US-Ha1/US-SRM, and a new
  Supplemental Figure S8 compares Figures 2/5a/6's main results screened vs unscreened directly
  — the differences are visually small at the skewness-third level (see S8).

## 4. Southern Hemisphere season-year join

`annual_gpp`, `annual_nep` and `annual_mean_ta` for the 16 Southern-Hemisphere sites
(`location_lat<0`) are now summed/averaged over the Jul(y)–Jun(y+1) season year, not the
same-numbered calendar year (`EXP_CFG_SH_SEASON_YEAR=TRUE`), feeding directly into
`carbon_gain_data.csv` and `temperature_sensitivity_data.csv` — this is now how the main
`carbon_gain_pooled.csv`/`temperature_sensitivity_pooled.csv` tables are built, not a side
comparison. 134 calendar-year SH site-year rows become 86 season-year rows (48 dropped: the
season-year window needs both calendar years y and y+1 in the site's own passing-year list,
which isn't always true at the edges of a site's record).

Session 1's check 3 already found this mismatch changes pooled slopes only marginally (r=0.92–0.995
between season-year and calendar-year values); consistent with that, the headline carbon-gain
numbers (item 1 vs a22e893, below) move by amounts of the same order as item 1's site-set and
item 2's screening changes combined — there is no indication the SH fix alone drives any of the
larger headline shifts.

## 5. Temperature sensitivity: window-average predictor for GSL

GSL's temperature-sensitivity predictor (Figure 6a) is now the **average of the start-window and
end-window 60-day pre-means** (`ta_window_avg_z17`/`_t95`), not the annual mean. Start and end
keep their existing 60-day pre-window means unchanged. The annual-mean-TA version is still
computed and written to `tables/temperature_sensitivity_pooled_annualta.csv`/
`temperature_sensitivity_data.csv`'s `annual_mean_ta` column — **table only, not plotted**.

**This is the largest single headline change in this session**, including a sign flip:

| skewness third | GSL~annual-mean-TA (a22e893) | GSL~window-avg-TA (new, Figure 6a) |
|---|---|---|
| low | 6.91 ± 1.03 | 4.53 ± 0.79 |
| middle | 7.68 ± 0.90 | 4.64 ± 0.79 |
| high | **3.42 ± 1.19** | **-1.99 ± 1.16** |

The high-skewness third's GSL~temperature slope flips from positive (warmer → longer season) to
negative (warmer → shorter season) once temperature is measured right around the season's own
start/end windows instead of over the whole calendar year. This is a genuine methods change, not
noise: the annual mean includes 10+ months of summer/dry-season temperature that is irrelevant to
when the season starts and ends for a short-season (high-skewness) site, diluting or reversing
the seasonal-boundary signal. **This result should be checked against the start/end-window
sensitivities in the same panel (which did not flip sign) before treating it as a finding** — it
is flagged in the limitations section below.

## 7. Trends: % of mean GSL per decade, and a trend-size detectability sweep

`trend_pooled.csv` gains `mean_gsl_subset` and `slope_pct_per_decade` columns
(`100 * slope_days_per_yr * 10 / mean_gsl_subset`) alongside the existing days/yr slope — e.g. the
Z17 trend by third: low 0.70 d/yr (2.58%/decade), middle 0.81 d/yr (3.56%/decade), high 0.05 d/yr
(0.25%/decade). The years-to-detect-the-**observed**-trend labels are dropped from Figure 6b
entirely (table column `years_to_detect_median` kept for reference, just not plotted). In their
place, a new table (`years_to_detect_sweep.csv`) and Figure 6c sweep trend **sizes** from 0.1 to
1.0 days/yr (Weatherhead et al. 1998, 95% confidence/80% power) at Z17 and T95, by third and
group, marking 0.25 (typical) and 0.5 (strong) days/yr. Representative numbers at Z17: a 0.25
d/yr trend needs ~17 years to detect for the low/middle thirds (26/22 sites), ~33 years for the
high third (fewer, noisier sites); a 0.5 d/yr trend needs ~17/17/33 years respectively.

Z17/T95's pooled trend values themselves are essentially unchanged from a22e893 at the subset
level that doesn't involve mixed forest (group-level trends for grassland, evergreen forest,
deciduous forest and woody-grass mosaic are numerically **identical** to a22e893 to the displayed
precision — these groups have essentially no winter-screened days, so neither item 1's site-set
change nor item 2's screen touches them); by-third trends move by a few hundredths of a day/yr
(e.g. Z17, third_middle: 0.746→0.811 d/yr), consistent with the modest site-set and screening
changes.

## 8. Bounds check (S2), redesigned

Only QC-failing days (fallback QC < 0.25, or NA, in a passing year) whose gap-filled GPP lies
within ±2% of P99 of the threshold being tested are now treated as ambiguous; GSL is reported
with all of them counted above (`gsl_upper`) and all counted below (`gsl_lower`). A failing day
far from the threshold keeps its own reported value either way — unlike a22e893, where *every*
QC-failing day counted as ambiguous regardless of how far its actual value was from the
threshold.

- Spread (`gsl_upper - gsl_lower`) drops from a 0–14 range (a22e893, = `n_fail_days` exactly) to a
  **0–9 range, mean 0.15–0.16 days** (`tables/bounds_check_pooled.csv`) — only 243 (Z17) / 254
  (T95) near-threshold day-instances out of 1587 site-years' worth of failing days in total.
- Because the near-threshold window is keyed to the threshold's own absolute value, Z17 and T95
  **no longer produce identical spread distributions** (a22e893's did, by construction, since
  `n_fail_days` never depended on which threshold was tested) — Supplemental Figure S2's caption
  is updated to say so; in practice the two distributions are still very close (both dominated by
  spread=0) because the two thresholds are close in absolute terms at most sites.
- The bounds check is now a much tighter, more realistic statement: QC failure alone creates GSL
  ambiguity only for the small number of days whose value actually sits near the threshold, not
  for every poor-QC day regardless of magnitude.

## What limits these results

- **Item 5's sign flip (high-skewness-third GSL~temperature slope) is the single result in this
  session most worth independent scrutiny.** It follows directly and mechanically from the
  predictor change (window-average vs annual-mean temperature), but a sign flip in a headline
  climate-sensitivity number deserves a second look before it goes in the paper, e.g. checking
  whether it is driven by a small number of short-season sites with unusual window-average
  temperatures.
- **Figure 4's "two sites" turned out to be one site at two thresholds** (AU-Ync) — the report
  above flags this rather than silently relabeling the prompt's wording as correct.
- **Figure 6 is now at the PeerJ page-height cap** (240 mm, 4 stacked panels) — there is no room
  left in this figure for further additions without either dropping a panel or moving the new
  panel c to a supplementary figure.
- **S8's carbon-gain panel (b) uses a lighter-weight per-site OLS comparison**, not the full
  pooled mixed model — adequate to see that the screen's effect on the carbon-gain slope is small
  at the skewness-third level, but not a rigorous pooled-model comparison.
- **The near-threshold bounds-check window (±2% of P99, item 8) is a config value
  (`EXP_CFG_BOUNDS_NEAR_PCT`), not independently derived** — chosen as instructed, not tuned or
  validated against an external benchmark.
- **The winter screen's network-wide reach (120 of 153 sites, 45,236 days) is much larger than
  session 1's US-Ha1/DBF-MF-focused estimate**, because it is unrestricted by month or by
  "already above a GSL threshold" — this is expected from the literal criterion given, not an
  error, but it means the screen is now a materially bigger methods decision for the whole
  network than session 1's framing suggested.
